# USIR v4: Measured Results on Real Japanese Prose and 8B Generators

## Abstract
This paper replaces the v2/v3 camera-ready manuscripts. A provenance audit (Phase 34) found that none of their
headline tables was measured on a real model or real text: Tables 1 and 4 were computed on synthetic corpora whose
generator was calibrated to target AUC ranges, and Tables 2 and 3 were produced by a mock simulator. Those tables are
retracted. Every number in this version is generated by `scripts/build_paper_v4.py` from result JSONs of runs on
real Aozora Bunko text and on real 8B models (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Swallow-8B) on Colab A100/L4 GPUs, and is re-checked by
`scripts/verify_paper_numbers.py`. Sample sizes are small (6 plot-seed groups per model, 46 works in the atlas); we
report counts and means and do not claim statistical significance.

## 2. Measurement instrument on real text (Aozora Bunko atlas)
Corpus: 46 public-domain works by 10 authors (Aozora Bunko), 600-character windows with a 300-character
stride. Authors are labels of the atlas only; no author or character name enters any feature extractor.

**Leave-one-work-out (LOWO) author identification.** Each work is held out, the class model is refit on the
remaining works, and the held-out work is assigned to the nearest class (chance 0.1). The Fisher model uses
class-balanced within-class scatter, ridge 1e-2 fixed before evaluation (`fit_fisher`).

| Instrument | LOWO top-1 | LOWO top-3 | Tanizaki (hits/works) | Sakaguchi (hits/works) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3-mode GMM genome (Phase 26, quantile/scaled) | 0.522 | 0.761 | 0/5 | 0/4 |
| Fisher LDA on 24D style features | 0.565 | 0.783 | 0/5 | 0/4 |
| Fisher LDA on 7 orthographic statistics | 0.326 | 0.717 | 0/5 | 0/4 |
| Fisher LDA on 24D + 7 orthographic | 0.522 | 0.804 | 0/5 | 1/4 |
| Fisher LDA on 27 extended orthographic | 0.609 | 0.848 | 1/5 | 1/4 |
| Fisher LDA on 24D + 27 extended orthographic (adopted) | 0.674 | 0.870 | 1/5 | 1/4 |

The plan's target (top-1 ≥ 0.70, and non-zero hits for Tanizaki and Sakaguchi) is not reached: the adopted model
reaches the value above, with one hit each for the two authors that previously had none. The ridge sensitivity
(`p30_reg_sensitivity.json`) gives top-1 between 0.652 and 0.696 for reg ∈ [1e-3, 1e-1]; the larger values are
not adopted because choosing reg after seeing LOWO accuracy would select on the evaluation.

**Phase 36: narrative-channel statistics.** Dialogue spans 「…」 enter the sentence-length denominator of the
orthographic statistics, and 600-character windows cut long sentences. Over whole texts the narrative (dialogue
removed) mean sentence length is 68.8 characters for Tanizaki and
45.3 for Akutagawa, while the window-level geometric means are
38.4 and 40.9, i.e. the order is reversed inside windows. Four narrative-channel statistics per
window (log mean and log 90th-percentile narrative sentence length with dialogue replaced by one placeholder and
boundary fragments dropped, commas per narrative sentence, dialogue character ratio) were added; the variant and
reg = 1e-2 were fixed before the run (`p36_lowo_narrative.json`).

| Instrument | LOWO top-1 | LOWO top-3 | Tanizaki | Sakaguchi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24D + 27 extended (Phase 30 re-run) | 31/46 = 0.674 | 0.870 | 1/5 | 1/4 |
| 4 narrative-channel statistics alone | 17/46 = 0.370 | 0.761 | 1/5 | 1/4 |
| 27 extended + 4 narrative | 29/46 = 0.630 | 0.870 | 1/5 | 1/4 |
| 24D + 27 extended + 4 narrative (pre-specified) | 34/46 = 0.739 | 0.913 | 3/5 | 1/4 |

Ridge sensitivity of the pre-specified variant (hits out of 46): 1e-3: 32, 3e-3: 32,
1e-2: 34, 3e-2: 34, 1e-1: 34. The variant was designed after the Phase 30 diagnostics of
the same 46 works, so this is not a held-out test; the improvement is three works. The decoder's Fisher metric
(`fisher_v1.json`, used for ΔF in §3) is unchanged.

**Window-level separability on real text** (Akutagawa A vs Tanizaki B, Akutagawa A vs Dazai C): ROC-AUC of the
instrument's score difference over 600-character windows of the two authors' works.

| Pair | ΔLL (GMM genome) AUC | Cohen's d | ΔF (Fisher) AUC | Cohen's d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A vs B | 0.686 | 0.419 | 0.737 | 0.859 |
| A vs C | 0.777 | 0.404 | 0.922 | 1.726 |

The GMM log-likelihood difference ΔLL used as the steering target in Phase 27 is a weak instrument on real text;
the Fisher score difference ΔF is used as the primary instrument from Phase 31 on.

## 3. Long-form generation with author steering (8B, ~2,000 characters per story)
### 3.1 Phase 27: genome steering through the GMM metric (negative result)
Two scripts × two seeds per model; ΔLL_AB = GMM log-likelihood(A) − (B) of the output, averaged over windows.

| Generator | new_ctrl ΔLL_AB | A-steered ΔLL_AB | B-steered ΔLL_AB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B | 6.52 | 6.61 | 7.35 |
| Qwen3-Swallow-8B | 2.80 | 2.28 | 2.25 |

The A- and B-steered outputs did not separate in the intended direction. Two causes were identified afterwards:
the GMM instrument's low separability on real text (§2), and the style term being active only in the Climax
paragraph and the paragraph after it (`climax_only_steering`).

### 3.2 Phase 31: proposal derived from measured orthography + Fisher-direction particle weight
Two changes couple the author statistics to the decoder (Clause-level twisted SMC, 4 particles):
(i) the steady-rhythm band, comma target and kanji bias are derived from the author's measured orthographic
statistics, amplified around the atlas mean with κ = 2; (ii) a particle-weight term
γ⟨v̂_author, F(prefix + candidate) − μ̄⟩ in the Fisher space of §2, active in every paragraph.
The plan's literal bands (Tanizaki 42–115 characters with many commas) contradict the measured statistics
(Tanizaki writes slightly longer sentences with fewer commas than Akutagawa); the derived values were used.
Forbidden-word and calendar-year bans were added (soft logit penalty −15).

**Phase 31b (Qwen3-8B, A/B re-run).** The steady band held only the single-token "。"; the vocabulary has
40 further tokens containing a sentence end (e.g. merged "た。"), which bypassed the hold. Extending the hold to

| Condition | ΔF_AB | ΔLL_AB | mean sentence length | commas / 100 chars |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Mean sentence length does not separate. The Swallow B-steered station stories contained kanji-numeral calendar

### 3.3 Phase 36: narrower band and stronger sentence-end biases
Pre-registered change (steered conditions): steady band 0.8–1.3 μ* (A 34–55, B 50–82 characters), sentence-end hold
−18 below the band and push +8 above it, comma cap round(c* + 2), and a calendar-year penalty of −10⁴ in all conditions.
Success criterion fixed before the run: mean sentence length B − A ≥ 10 characters and B > new_ctrl.
Qwen3-8B, 6 plot-seed groups, A100 (2964 s of generation):

| Condition | mean sentence length | commas / 100 chars | ΔF_AB | ΔLL_AB | coinages | unfinished paragraphs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| new_ctrl | 28.2 | 5.40 | 9.24 | 7.28 | 0 | 0 |
| A (Akutagawa, μ* 42.5) | 41.0 | 6.99 | 14.99 | -0.24 | 0 | 0 |
| B (Tanizaki, μ* 63.1) | 46.8 | 5.26 | 6.32 | -2.82 | 0 | 0 |

B − A in mean sentence length: mean 5.75 characters (range 1.2–10.6); B longer than A in 6 of 6 groups and
longer than new_ctrl in 6 of 6. The 10-character criterion is **not met**. A reached 96 % of its target μ*, B only
74 %; the per-paragraph breakdown (short-sentence pulse paragraphs, 20-token clause cap) was not measured in this run.

Swallow station (3 seeds): mean sentence length new_ctrl 35.8, B 54.1. Four-digit numeral runs: 1 in B,
0 in new_ctrl, so the zero-year criterion is **not met**. Cause, checked on the Qwen3 tokenizer: "〇" (U+3007)
has no single token and is split into two byte-level tokens that decode to U+FFFD individually, so the per-token
digit table used by the mask never contains it; years without "〇" (e.g. 一九六八) are tokens whose decoded surfaces contain the digits
and are masked. The penalty value is not the cause.

**Post-hoc split by paragraph type (Phase 37, no new generation).** The genome band acts only on steady paragraphs
(director well ≠ 2); the Climax paragraph (well 2) uses the pulse profile in every condition, including new_ctrl.
Sentence lengths (same splitter as above), number of sentences in parentheses:

| Model | Condition | steady paragraphs | Climax paragraph | Climax share of sentences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B | new_ctrl | 29.6 (318) | 21.7 (82) | 20.5 % |
| Qwen3-8B | gA | 54.9 (180) | 18.5 (113) | 38.6 % |
| Qwen3-8B | gB | 71.3 (138) | 17.6 (116) | 45.7 % |
| Swallow | new_ctrl | 39.1 (134) | 24.8 (41) | 23.4 % |
| Swallow | gB | 79.9 (69) | 19.2 (52) | 43.0 % |

In steady paragraphs B − A is 16.35 characters on average (range 12.6–21.1; 6 of 6 groups ≥ 10). The Climax
paragraph has short sentences and therefore many of them, so it dominates the whole-text mean. This split was chosen
after seeing the result; the pre-registered whole-text criterion remains **not met**, and the split is reported as
an explanation, not as a substitute. Both steered conditions overshoot their targets in steady paragraphs (A:
76 of 180 sentences above the band maximum); the sentence-end bias is evaluated once per clause, so a clause
that starts inside the band can run past it. For comparison, Phase 31b steady paragraphs gave A 39.6 and B 42.5.
An optional in-band ramp of the '。' bias (`band_ramp`, default 0) and a mask for byte-split "〇" were added in Phase 37;
they are unit-tested only and have not been run on a model.

## 4. LLM-as-judge
### 4.1 Phase 28: generative pairwise judge (position-biased)
Cross-judging (Swallow judges Qwen3 outputs and vice versa), 5 axes, both presentation orders. The fraction of
axis decisions that chose the first-presented story was 0.78 (Swallow as judge) and 0.78
(Qwen3 as judge); the generative judge is therefore not usable as a measurement of condition differences.

### 4.2 Phase 32: logit-debiased pairwise judge
One forward pass per order; the prompt ends with the answer stem and the judge's next-token log-probabilities of
"1" and "2" are read: s(A,B) = ln P(1) − ln P(2), preference = (s(A,B) − s(B,A))/2, position bias =
(s(A,B) + s(B,A))/2 (nats). A constant position bias cancels exactly; a content × position interaction does not.

| Setting | Pair (a vs b) | mean preference (whole text) | mean position bias |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3 outputs, Swallow judge | new_ctrl vs min | +0.188 | 2.00 |
| Qwen3 outputs, Swallow judge | gA vs new_ctrl | +0.068 | 2.05 |
| Qwen3 outputs, Swallow judge | gB vs new_ctrl | -0.005 | 2.02 |
| Qwen3 outputs, Swallow judge | gC vs new_ctrl | -0.104 | 2.25 |
| Qwen3 outputs, Swallow judge | gA vs gB | +0.115 | 2.24 |
| Swallow outputs, Qwen3 judge | new_ctrl vs min | -0.437 | 6.38 |
| Swallow outputs, Qwen3 judge | gA vs new_ctrl | -0.115 | 6.49 |
| Swallow outputs, Qwen3 judge | gB vs new_ctrl | -0.177 | 6.70 |
| Swallow outputs, Qwen3 judge | gC vs new_ctrl | -0.328 | 6.49 |
| Swallow outputs, Qwen3 judge | gA vs gB | +0.224 | 6.47 |

Probability mass on the two answer tokens: 0.621 (Swallow judge) and 0.017 (Qwen3 judge); the Qwen3 judge mostly
predicts other tokens at the answer position, so its values are unreliable. The position bias exceeds the
debiased preferences by roughly an order of magnitude, and the signs disagree between judges and between the
whole-text and paragraph-aligned modes. No quality difference between steered and unsteered stories is claimed.

## 5. Mid-layer steering and GRPO-LoRA (Phase C)
### 5.1 Phase 28: raw continuation prompts
Qwen3-8B (bf16, A100). With raw literary continuation prompts the base model was in the Deadzone in 0 of 12
outputs, so the former claim of "Deadzone escape" could not be tested on that set; steering at larger α increased
the Deadzone count, and the calibrated direction did not differ from a norm-matched random direction.

### 5.2 Phase 33: Deadzone-inducing instruct prompts
Qwen3-8B (bf16, A100), 12 explanatory instruct prompts (no bullets or headings, polite register, about 800 characters),
400 new tokens at T=0.8. The Deadzone is the QDA phase classifier (`Phase_I_Deadzone`); CV_L < 0.28 is reported as a
second definition. Both were fixed in the driver before the run.

The base model was in the Deadzone in 7/12 outputs (CV definition 8/12),
so this prompt set does induce the Deadzone.

Steering adds a direction at layer 23 of 36, built from independent per-element ridge maps (5-fold CV R² mean 0.658;
maximum off-diagonal |cos| between element columns 0.934, i.e. the element directions are far from orthogonal).
The control is a random direction with matched norm. "Degenerate" means distinct character-bigram ratio < 0.5, a
threshold chosen after inspecting the outputs (repetition loops). Low reference PPL in degenerate rows reflects
repetition, not quality.

| inject | α | calibrated Deadzone | random Deadzone | calibrated degenerate | random degenerate | calibrated ref PPL | random ref PPL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 0.05 | 5/12 | 8/12 | 1 | 0 | 2.04 | 1.61 |
| all | 0.15 | 8/12 | 5/12 | 12 | 3 | 1.51 | 2.19 |
| all | 0.3 | 12/12 | 8/12 | 12 | 12 | 1.22 | 1.72 |
| last | 0.05 | 5/12 | 6/12 | 0 | 0 | 1.69 | 1.57 |
| last | 0.15 | 1/12 | 10/12 | 3 | 0 | 2.70 | 1.69 |
| last | 0.3 | 7/12 | 6/12 | 12 | 0 | 1.80 | 2.19 |

The largest difference is at last-position injection with α = 0.15. It comes with a higher reference PPL and with
some degenerate outputs; at α = 0.3 both directions collapse or lose the effect, so the dose response is not monotone.
The base reference PPL is 1.55. We therefore do not claim that calibrated steering resolves the
Deadzone.

GRPO-LoRA (r = 8, 40 steps, 256-token rollouts, multiplicative physical reward times a reference-PPL barrier):
the mean reward over the first 10 steps was 0.081 and over the last 10 steps 0.077, with maximum KL 0.0054.
The reward did not rise. The merged adapter was in the Deadzone in 8/12 outputs, versus
7/12 for the base model, so it did not reduce the Deadzone. Decode latency (64 tokens × 5 repetitions):
original 54.10 ms/token,
merged 54.01 ms/token,
unmerged 66.43 ms/token. Merging removes the
adapter overhead, but this adapter has no measured effect to deliver.

### 5.3 Phase 36: unit-norm direction, smaller α, shaped GRPO reward
Same prompts, seed and layer as §5.2. The steering direction is normalised, û = VΔq/‖VΔq‖ (‖VΔq‖ was 1.60 before
normalisation; the random control is normalised too), injected at the last position with α ∈ {0.08, 0.12}, which
corresponds to α 0.05 / 0.075 of §5.2. Criteria fixed before the run: calibrated Deadzone < random, no degenerate
output, reference PPL within base + 0.5, merged-LoRA Deadzone < base. The base model gave 7/12
(ref PPL 1.551), identical to §5.2.

| α | calibrated Deadzone | random Deadzone | calibrated CV < 0.28 | random CV < 0.28 | calibrated degenerate | random degenerate | calibrated ref PPL | random ref PPL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.08 | 5/12 | 7/12 | 5/12 | 9/12 | 0 | 0 | 1.704 | 1.582 |
| 0.12 | 1/12 | 7/12 | 4/12 | 6/12 | 0 | 0 | 1.873 | 1.574 |

The three steering criteria are met at both α. At α = 0.12 the calibrated direction reaches the Deadzone count of
§5.2's α = 0.15 without degenerate outputs and at a lower reference PPL. With n = 12 and one seed series the Wilson
95 % intervals at α = 0.12 still overlap (calibrated upper bound 0.354, random lower bound 0.320), the CV-based definition
shows a smaller gap, and the reference PPL is above base; no significance is claimed.

GRPO with the shaped reward clip(CV_L/0.45, 0.05, 1.2) × cadence_alive × PPL barrier (all other settings as §5.2):
the reward is continuous and the within-group reward standard deviation stayed at 0.113 (first 10 steps) and
0.104 (last 10), but the mean reward did not rise (0.286 → 0.281; maximum KL 0.0254). The merged adapter was in
the Deadzone in 7/12 outputs, equal to base, so the merged-LoRA criterion is **not met**. Decode latency:
original 56.36 ms/token, merged
56.42, unmerged
69.48.

**Sensitivity to the truncated tail (Phase 37, re-analysis of the same texts).** Evaluation stops at 400 new tokens,
so many outputs end inside a sentence, and the extractor treats the fragment as the last sentence. Recomputing the
classifiers on each text with the fragment after the last sentence end removed (counts out of 12):

| Set | truncated | Deadzone | Deadzone, tail removed | CV < 0.28 | CV < 0.28, tail removed | contains a blank line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 6 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 2 |
| calibrated α 0.08 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 6 | 4 |
| random α 0.08 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 1 |
| calibrated α 0.12 | 8 | 1 | 5 | 4 | 7 | 4 |
| random α 0.12 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 6 | 9 | 4 |
| merged LoRA | 11 | 7 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 7 |

With the tail removed, calibrated α 0.12 is still below random α 0.12 and below base, but its Deadzone count rises
from 1 to 5, so its gaps to base and to random both shrink; part of the α 0.12 effect in the table above comes
from truncated fragments, and the steering result is weaker than that table suggests. cadence_alive is two-valued over all 72 evaluation texts (25 below 0.26,
0 between 0.26 and 0.65): the extractor's flat-slop gate (mean length > 40, CV_L < 0.28, and abstract fillers or
sluggish endings) subtracts 0.68 as a step. The merged adapter ended inside a sentence and used blank lines more often
than base. A reward that depends on the truncated tail and on this step is a plausible reason why GRPO did not learn
the intended rhythm, but the training rollouts were not stored, so this is not verified. The shaped reward now has
a `strip_tail` option (complete sentences only), unit-tested and not yet trained with.

## 6. Clean-room gate
Tier S (128 bytes) and Tier M (4096 bytes) style binaries pass `threeRoomGate.ts`: no raw text retained and no
contiguous ≥ 7-character overlap with the source. These are fixture tests in the TypeScript suite and a repository
scan (`scripts/scan_cleanroom_copyright.py`); they show that the gate rejects the fixture violations, not a
generalisation rate on held-out data.

## 7. Limitations
- Small n: 6 plot-seed groups per model and condition, 46 works in the atlas. No significance testing.
- The style instrument (Fisher LDA) and the steering term share the same feature space; ΔF improvements are
  partly by construction. ΔLL (a different instrument) and orthographic statistics are reported for that reason.
- LOWO top-1 is below 0.70 with the Phase 30 instrument; the Phase 36 variant exceeds it on the same 46 works
  that informed its design (no held-out author set).
- The 8B generative judge is position-biased; the logit-debiased judge cancels a constant position bias only.
- Coinage detection is a fixed regular-expression list, not a general detector.
- Phase C (§5.2, §5.3) uses 12 prompts and one seed series; the degeneracy threshold was set after inspection;
  neither 40-step GRPO run (multiplicative or shaped reward) raised the reward, so they say nothing about longer training.
- Phase 36 sentence length: B − A stayed below the pre-registered 10 characters; the calendar-year mask misses "〇",
  which the Qwen3 tokenizer splits into byte-level tokens.
- Phase 37 analyses (paragraph-type split, truncated-tail sensitivity) were chosen after seeing the Phase 36 results;
  the Phase 37 code changes (in-band ramp, byte-split "〇" mask, tail stripping in the reward) have unit tests only.

## Appendix A. Synthetic self-consistency checks (not evidence)
The former Tables 1 and 4 remain reproducible from `python/usir_py/v2/benchmark_v2_large_scale.py` and
`benchmark_v3_english_cross_linguistic.py`. Their corpora are generated by the repository itself, with separability
calibrated by construction; they test that the pipeline runs end to end and are not evidence of real-world accuracy.
